Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about what this project is, what it isn't, and how to use it responsibly.

Is this a medical device?

No.

Doctordrobe is an educational home-health analyzer. It is not FDA/CE/MDR cleared, it has not been clinically validated, and the sensor model ships trained on simulated chemistry until you collect real calibration samples. The reports are explicitly labeled not a medical device in the UI, the PDF export, and the code. Treat any value as a curiosity, never a diagnosis. Confirm clinically significant findings with a laboratory test and a clinician.

How accurate is it?

Out of the box: the SaliNet model is a Random Forest trained on simulated Beer-Lambert strip chemistry, with held-out R² around 0.56-0.92 depending on analyte. That measures the model against its own simulator, not against reality. The honest path to accuracy is the calibration workflow: collect labeled samples from control standards and retrain on real measurements.

Accuracy also depends on capture quality. The system grades each reading (good / fair / poor) from burst variance, light levels, and spectral residuals - a poor capture is flagged with a retake recommendation rather than reported with false confidence.

What happens to my data?

  • Reports are encrypted with Fernet before storage; list views only see a summary and risk label.
  • Passwords are salted + hashed with PBKDF2; session tokens are stored as SHA-256 digests.
  • Sharing a checkup is always opt-in and awards tokens - nothing is shared by default.
  • You can export everything (Vault) or delete your account (which cascades to all checkups).

Do I need to build the hardware?

To take real readings, yes - the backend refuses to fabricate data, and there is no simulated mode. But you can run the full stack and explore the API with a manually posted reading (see getting started). The hardware is ~$35-45 in parts and the build takes an evening (build guide).

Why these five biomarkers?

Saliva is a practical medium for home testing, and these five are all measurable colorimetrically with strip chemistry: glucose (glucose-oxidase), CRP (immunochromogenic), cortisol (ELISA-type), pH (mixed indicator), and secretory IgA (turbidimetric). They also happen to be a coherent health panel: metabolic (glucose, CRP), stress (cortisol), and mucosal immunity (sIgA). See analysis science for the chemistry.

Three RGB channels for five analytes?

Good question - it's an ill-posed inverse problem. The answer is in the science page: the system captures a burst of snapshots, deconvolves them together as one over-determined system, regularizes toward a prior (SaliNet or Beer-Lambert), and reports a per-marker identifiability confidence so you know when a number was not resolvable.

Why won't my ESP32 connect to Wi-Fi?

The ESP32 only supports 2.4 GHz networks. If your router broadcasts only 5 GHz, the device will never associate. See the hardware troubleshooting table.

How do I recover a lost password?

Password recovery isn't implemented yet - by design, because this is a self-hosted project. If you lose access, delete the account row (or database) and register again. This is a known gap for production use; see auth roadmap.

What are the tokens for?

A gamified incentive for the community data pool: sharing a checkup (opt-in) awards 5 tokens. They have no monetary value; the point is to make anonymized cohort data meaningful as the fleet grows.

How can I help?

See contributing. High-value contributions: real calibration datasets, better biology in the forward model, per-device API keys, password recovery, and clinical validation partnerships.
