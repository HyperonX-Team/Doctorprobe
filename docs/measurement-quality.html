Measurement quality

A ~$4 sensor held by a human reads a strip under varying light. Sometimes the capture is garbage. The system grades every reading and tells the user when to retake - instead of printing a confident-looking number from bad data.

The grades

GradeMeaningRecommended action
goodStable burst, healthy light levels, well-conditioned fit.None
fairUsable, with caveats (e.g. weak burst, elevated residual).review
poorNot trustworthy - retake with a fresh strip.retake_reading

What is graded

The verdict is computed in analyzer._measurement_quality from three families of signals:

Burst statistics

  • Snapshot count. Fewer than 3 snapshots → fair; a burst of ≥ 3 is recommended for a reliable read.
  • Per-channel coefficient of variation. > 15% variation between snapshots means the strip or lighting moved mid-scan → poor.

Absolute light levels

  • All channels < 35 → very little light reached the sensor → poor.
  • All channels > 245 → no colour reaction detected (blank or expired strip) → poor.

Spectral solver diagnostics

  • Reconstruction residual > 0.25 → lighting may have changed mid-scan → fair.
  • Condition number > 200 → channels weakly separated → fair.

How it surfaces

  • API - each checkup carries a plain quality_grade column for list views, and the decrypted report embeds the full quality object: grade, human-readable reasons[], and recommended_action.
  • Report page - a banner for fair/poor readings lists the reasons; poor shows "Recommended action: retake the reading".
  • PDF export - the quality verdict and reasons are printed in the clinician report.

Quality gates the risk score

Quality interacts with the biology in one more place: an out-of-range marker only contributes to the risk score (and only gets the "attention needed" message) when its spectral identifiability confidence clears the 0.5 gate. A marker the measurement couldn't resolve is never presented as a confident finding.

Why this matters

  • Honesty. Bad readings are flagged, not averaged into false confidence.
  • Longitudinal hygiene. Poor captures pollute trends; the grade lets users filter or retake.
  • Clinical defensibility. The PDF a clinician sees states exactly how trustworthy the capture was.

Improving your capture

Hold the strip flat and steady, keep ambient light consistent, use fresh strips, and take the reading within the reaction window. A blank-pad baseline (CAL BLANK) further stabilizes the device's optics - see firmware.
